# Five Felt Worlds

This reference distils the user-curated 24-work reading *他画的是感受，不是样子*. It does not classify works by subject, period, or icon. It gives a source photo one governing experience after its visual signals and relation have been read.

Use these worlds as perceptual lenses, not as templates, mood labels, or automatic compositions. They identify the pressure that must survive; the actual artistic trait and formal solution must be discovered from the individual source. Let the model's judgment move from the world's consequence to a source-specific act of enlargement, displacement, compression, suspension, eruption, or release.

The worlds are mutually exclusive at selection time. A painting may contain several moods, but choose the one whose loss would most damage the source’s meaning. It supplies a visual argument, not a stock motif.

## 激荡 — force moves through the field

**Signal pattern:** opposing directions, rising pressure, weather, wind, crowd flow, exposed distance, or an anchor resisting a larger movement.

**Visual consequence:** turn dispersed forms into a field with a current, then place one exposed, resisting, or receiving anchor against it. Scale and crop may change sharply.

**Do not borrow:** stars, crows, cypresses, roads, or storm patterns as signs of Van Gogh.

**Primary works:** 01 *The Starry Night*; 08 *Wheatfield with Crows*; 19 *Olive Trees*.

## 焦灼 — the relation cannot settle

**Signal pattern:** an inner conflict, blocked attention, unease, too-close contrast, trapped space, an unhealed break, or a colour relation that refuses rest.

**Visual consequence:** make tension local and consequential: press incompatible temperatures together, compress or interrupt space, turn marks against a form, or isolate the unstable point. Colour is one means, not the category itself.

**Do not confuse with:** 激荡. 激荡 is force crossing the whole field; 焦灼 is a relation that cannot find rest.

**Primary works:** 05 *Café Terrace at Night*; 06 *The Night Café*; 11 *Self-Portrait*; 12 *Self-Portrait with Bandaged Ear*; 13 *Portrait of Dr. Gachet*.

## 静谧 — calm remains alive

**Signal pattern:** repose, measured intervals, shelter, held attention, reflective distance, or an equilibrium that would be lost if made inert.

**Visual consequence:** hold a stable centre with restrained contrast, proportionate space, gentle directional echoes, and marks that continue quietly rather than surge. Calm is energy that has found proportion, not an empty image.

**Do not confuse with:** 生机. 静谧 maintains equilibrium; 生机 breaks outward from it.

**Primary works:** 04 *The Bedroom*; 07 *Starry Night Over the Rhône*; 14 *The Yellow House*; 15 *Wheat Field with Cypresses*; 16 *Portrait of Joseph Roulin*; 22 *The Siesta*; 23 *L’Arlésienne*.

## 困顿 — weight interrupts movement

**Signal pattern:** lowered energy, waiting, depletion, difficult attention, burden, a compressed body or room, a trace of use, or a relation unable to move forward.

**Visual consequence:** make weight, support, wear, and enclosing space visible. A hand, folded body, chair, threshold, empty surface, or close field can carry it. This is not a portrait or clinical-diagnosis category.

**Do not confuse with:** 焦灼. 困顿 is pressure endured or stalled; 焦灼 is tension that actively cannot settle.

**Primary works:** 10 *The Potato Eaters*; 21 *At Eternity’s Gate*; 24 *Van Gogh’s Chair*.

## 生机 — life presses outward

**Signal pattern:** opening, lift, emergence, ripeness, branching rhythm, labour becoming conviction, or concentrated vitality that wants more space.

**Visual consequence:** enlarge the living event, make rhythm branch or rise, let marks accumulate into outward movement, and give the active force room to expand. It can describe a person, a crowd, cloth, architecture, or light; it is not a plant category.

**Do not confuse with:** 激荡. 生机 is generative outward force; 激荡 is a field under directional pressure.

**Primary works:** 02 *Sunflowers*; 03 *Irises*; 09 *Almond Blossom*; 17 *The Sower*; 18 *The Mulberry Tree*; 20 *Still Life: Vase with Irises*.

## Selection sentence

Complete: “The source makes **[felt world]** visible because **[signals]** establish **[relation]**; the painting will reveal this by **[one visual action]**.”

If the sentence needs two felt worlds, the reading is not yet precise enough. Return to the signals and identify which relation is carrying the source’s actual centre of gravity.

The selected world is not the artwork. After choosing it, ask: “What is the source making me feel before I name what I see, and what formal behaviour would make that feeling larger than the depicted thing?” The answer may be a changed composition, scale, intensity, colour temperature, directional field, density, or silence. Do not force every source into the stock visual consequence listed above; use that consequence as a provocation, then make the most expressive source-grounded decision.
