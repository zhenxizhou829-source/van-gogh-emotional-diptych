# From Visual Signal to Painterly Action

Use this guide inside the perceptual reconstruction treatment, after reading the source’s signals and selecting its single felt world, but before selecting a board. It converts evidence into a source-specific artistic decision; it does not provide a visual template.

## Make the felt relation larger than the depicted thing

The first job is not to describe the object more beautifully. It is to find the formal behaviour that carries the feeling:

- turn a gesture into a compositional vector;
- turn distance or proximity into scale, crop, overlap, or negative space;
- turn colour contrast into temperature, pressure, or a change in emotional intensity;
- turn repetition into a field, rhythm, or enclosing force;
- turn material, light, or absence into the image's active atmosphere.

Choose the most expressive combination for this source. Composition is usually the primary lever; intensity, colour, density, direction, contour, and quiet can amplify it. The goal is an artistic trait specific to the image, not a generic “emotional” treatment or a literal object with decorative brushwork.

An artistic trait is a visible behaviour, not an adjective. Replace “poetic,” “beautiful,” “dreamlike,” or “emotional” with what the image does: it compresses, hovers, radiates, buckles, encloses, dissolves, interrupts, or breaks outward. The chosen behaviour must govern the reconstruction, not merely decorate its surface.

## What the positive reconstructions do

Use the source's latent relation as the compositional subject. In the positive examples:

- “one red flower inside a mass of green leaves” is not handled as a centred portrait with a flower repainted. The red accent becomes the structural centre of the painted field; the figure recedes into, is framed by, or is reorganised by the surrounding foliage, so the experience of concentrated colour inside abundance becomes the image.
- “a monumental stone mass beside a light veil and a small figure” is not handled as the same portrait in front of the same sculpture. The stone is given a new dominant scale and position while the veil and figure become a lighter counterweight below or against it, so heaviness and delicacy become a spatial relation.

In both cases, the painting keeps enough identity for recognition but changes the visual order that the camera imposed. This is the standard: enlarge the relation, not the object inventory. The new composition must make the viewer feel the source's inner contrast before they finish naming its things.

## Read signals as verbs, not nouns

| What the photo visibly does | Ask | Translate into paint |
|---|---|---|
| A face, hand, object, light, or gap holds the eye | What must receive the first look? | Give it the clearest silhouette, the most exact colour relation, and the most specific marks. |
| A gesture points, folds, leans, reaches, or withdraws | What relation does the body enact? | Let contour, body mass, and stroke direction continue that gesture; omit anatomy that does not carry it. |
| A large and small form, near and far form, or body and field are in tension | Who or what has power here? | Change scale, spacing, crop, or depth until that tension becomes legible. |
| Repetition, paths, water, foliage, traffic, fabric, or light moves in one direction | What force crosses the scene? | Merge repetitions into a directional field and give it one meaningful anchor or interruption. |
| A warm/cool, light/dark, or saturated/muted contrast changes the scene | What emotional relation is colour already making? | Assign colour roles; use temperature, area, and adjacency to make the relation spatially consequential. |
| An empty place, reflection, worn surface, partial view, or trace remains | What is present through absence? | Expand, protect, or quiet that cue only when it clarifies the central relation. |

## Turn the chosen world into one argument

- **激荡:** use directional conflict, broad current, and a resisting anchor. Let the field move more than the anchor.
- **焦灼:** compress or interrupt the unstable relation. Localise colour conflict and make marks press, split, or stop where the tension lives.
- **静谧:** use measured intervals, restraint, a stable centre, and continuing marks. Keep enough low-pressure space for calm to register.
- **困顿:** make weight visible in support, lowered energy, worn material, folded mass, or enclosing quiet. Do not sentimentalise or diagnose.
- **生机:** let a living rhythm rise, branch, gather, or spread beyond its former scale. Preserve room for expansion.

## Universal painterly constraints

1. Build form from neighbouring hues, selective contour, and directional marks; keep shadows chromatic.
2. Change marks whenever material, force, or attention changes. Never cover every region with the same swirl, impasto, or intensity.
3. Retain a quieter field. Density and contrast matter because they are selective.
4. A Van Gogh-associated cue requires a source trigger, a structural role, and subordinate scale. It may not act as quotation or proof of style.
5. If detailed photographic information does not clarify the relation, remove it. If an invented element cannot be traced to a source cue, remove it.

## Test

At normal display size, the painting should reveal the felt relation and its source-specific artistic trait before the viewer inventories its objects. If it reads first as an unchanged photograph under oil texture, or as a stock mood applied to a recognisable object, rebuild the treatment rather than adding more texture.
