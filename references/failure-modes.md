# Failure Modes

Read this only when a generated interpretation feels technically painterly but conceptually uncertain.

## Semantic evidence mistaken for scene fidelity

**Symptom:** The painting keeps the source photo's crop, viewpoint, pose, scale, background geometry, and object positions because the brief says to preserve identity, setting, or anchors.

**Why it fails:** The source has been treated as a camera template. Semantic fidelity means preserving the relation that makes the image matter; it does not mean preserving the photographed arrangement that happened to record it.

**Repair:** Retain no more than one to three semantic anchors. State which camera decisions are being discarded, then write an independent new scene around the dominant force and its counterweight. The new image must be recognisably of the same felt event without being alignable to the photograph.

## Mood label without artistic reconstruction

**Symptom:** The brief correctly names a felt world such as 静谧 or 激荡, but the painting remains a literal portrait or scene with a pleasant palette. It communicates a category of mood rather than this source's particular tension and artistic character.

**Why it fails:** Perceptual translation has stopped at classification. The felt world is a starting lens; it is not a finished artistic proposition.

**Repair:** Return to the integrated treatment. State what the source makes the viewer feel before naming the objects, then find the source-specific formal behaviour that enlarges it: a changed spatial relation, scale, intensity, colour temperature, directional field, density, contour, or silence. Push past the first literal solution, but keep the causal path from source signal to visual consequence.

## Board-led generation

**Symptom:** The chosen board's palette, motif, or familiar Van Gogh-like arrangement determines the image before the source has been understood. The result is attractive but could have been made from many unrelated photos.

**Why it fails:** A brushwork reference has become a meaning and composition template. It reverses the required order and makes the source subordinate to style.

**Repair:** Return to Gate 1 and Gate 2 of the private brief. State the source's signals, core relation, and necessary evidence; write the integrated perceptual reconstruction treatment first. Select one board only afterward, and translate it into actions of mark, colour, rhythm, density, and atmosphere. Remove any layout, object, or motif that came from the board rather than the source.

## Minimal-change trap

**Symptom:** The painting changes colour and adds visible strokes but keeps the original crop, subject scale, eye entry, centre of gravity, background intervals, and main silhouette. It may look more painterly while the image argument remains unchanged.

**Why it fails:** “Preserve source evidence” has been misread as “preserve the camera composition.” The work has no reconstructed point of view and no surprise.

**Repair:** Treat minimal change as the minimum evidence needed after a genuine redesign, never as a target. Require one decisive spatial reconstruction plus at least one consequential change in scale, intensity, colour, density, or directional pressure, and name one source-traceable surprise point. If the result could be aligned over the source, regenerate from the integrated screenplay-like treatment rather than adding more texture.

## Object enlargement without relation enlargement

**Symptom:** One flower, face, statue, or other object has been made bigger or more detailed, but the surrounding visual order is unchanged. The result magnifies a thing without making the source's inner contrast or emotional pressure more legible.

**Why it fails:** Scale has been used as an effect instead of as a relational decision. The painting still asks the viewer to admire the object rather than feel what the object is doing inside the scene.

**Repair:** Rebuild the surrounding hierarchy with the enlarged element: make the field enclose, oppose, recede from, or yield to it; change the counterweight, depth, negative space, and directional flow. Ask what relation is being enlarged—colour within abundance, weight against lightness, exposure against shelter, or another source-grounded contrast—and make that relation govern the whole composition.

## Literal scene remapping

**Symptom:** The painting preserves the photograph's camera view object-for-object: the same subject size, pose, intervals, background geometry, foreground detail, and compositional balance survive, with only brush texture and altered colour added.

**Why it fails:** It treats the photo as a layout template. The subject remains a photographed object, rather than becoming the centre of a felt relation. An interpretive title then becomes a caption placed on top of an unchanged visual statement.

**Repair:** Return to the private brief. Retain only the source anchors required by the felt proposition. Then make the interpretive leap legible: rebuild scale, placement, depth, supporting forms, directional movement, and quiet space when needed; otherwise use colour, density, mark direction, selective enlargement, or a revealed latent cue. Do not settle for a small adjustment when the source's emotional logic calls for a large re-reading.

## Decorative symbolic addition

**Symptom:** A new bird, star, flower, halo, path, or other expressive form is added because it resembles a Van Gogh motif or makes the image look poetic.

**Why it fails:** Association replaces interpretation. The added form has no causal role in the source or in the painting's visual structure.

**Repair:** State the causal chain: `source cue → implied relation or force → visual consequence`. Remove the form unless every arrow is defensible. When the consequence is essential, let the title echo its relation or force.

## Title-led interpretation

**Symptom:** The title asserts a relationship that the painted hierarchy, colour, marks, and space do not make perceptible.

**Why it fails:** Naming is being used as a substitute for painting.

**Repair:** Either make the relation visible through the visual argument or replace the title with one grounded in the image that exists.

## Surface-style portrait

**Symptom:** The face, clothing, and accessories retain camera-specific information while the surface receives visible oil strokes.

**Why it fails:** This is cosmetic restyling, not painterly reconstruction.

**Repair:** Reduce the subject to its relation-bearing planes, mass, pose, gesture, and surrounding pressure. Reconstruct it with directional marks and chromatic form; remove literal cosmetic and photographic residue.
